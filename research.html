Research themes

What we study

CO₂ sublimation, agglomeration and clogging

We investigate solid CO₂ sublimation in open gaseous environments and confined channels, including agglomeration and clogging of flow passages. Our research examines coupled heat and mass transfer, moving interfaces, particle size distribution, and solid-gas flow dynamics.

Understanding these phenomena is important for developing effective spray cooling technologies, ultra-low-temperature refrigeration, and maintaining reliable operation of heat exchangers for thermal management application.

CO₂ Desublimation and Melting

We study the formation of solid CO₂ from gas mixtures, including deposition on cold surfaces, and particle formation in cryogenic spray systems. We also investigate melting of the formed CO₂ frost under pressurized conditions. Our research examines how heat and mass transfer, frost morphology, and cold surface texture influence these processes.

Understanding these mechanisms is essential for developing efficient cryogenic carbon capture, storage systems, and processes involving the handling and recovery of solid CO₂.

Heat Transfer at Supercritical Pressures

We investigate heat transfer and fluid flow near the pseudo-critical region, where strong variations in thermophysical properties give rise to complex transport behaviour. Our research focuses on pseudo-boiling-like phenomena, heat-transfer enhancement and deterioration, and the influence of buoyancy and flow conditions.

Understanding these mechanisms is important for designing reliable high-pressure thermal systems, including heat exchangers, and energy conversion applications.

Transport in Porous Media

We investigate heat and mass transport through porous materials, focusing on the influence of pore structure, effective thermal conductivity, diffusion, permeability, and evolving material morphology.

Understanding these coupled transport processes is essential for improving heat storage systems and thermal technologies involving complex material structures.

Ongoing and recent projects

Funded research activities

NWO Veni 2027–2030

Unveiling the physics of CO₂ desublimation: advancing cryogenic carbon capture through fundamental phase-change studies

This Veni project investigates the fundamental physics of CO₂ desublimation on cold surfaces under controlled gas-composition, pressure, temperature, and flow conditions. The project aims to understand solid CO₂ layer growth, morphology evolution, thermal resistance, and boundary-layer transport in order to support the development of predictive models and improved cryogenic carbon capture concepts.

Industry-funded project 2025

Energy and mass balance of an existing cryogenic carbon capture unit

This industry-funded project focused on evaluating the energy and mass balance of an existing cryogenic carbon capture unit. The work supported the assessment of system-level performance, material flows, cooling requirements, and opportunities for improving the operation of cryogenic CO₂ separation technology.
